document.

\section{Summary of what this report establishes}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Observed.} The original context-holdout protocol produced
  $\rsq \approx 0.93$--$0.97$ on the three general-shelf-life specialists
  and $\rsq$ between $0.59$ and $-0.10$ on the three safety specialists.
\item \textbf{Observed.} $100\%$ of test rows in every specialist came
  from a generation rule also present in training; context overlap was
  zero.
\item \textbf{Observed.} Reducing model capacity, changing $K$,
  aggregating categories, and removing the validation set each failed to
  change the scores materially.
\item \textbf{Observed.} Under leave-one-rule-out, the production
  ensembles retain median fold $\rsq$ of $0.72$--$0.79$ while linear and
  instance-based models fall below zero.
\item \textbf{Verified explanation.} Hard/general's collapse is caused by
  one held-out rule carrying the only substantial representation of the
  clostridia mechanism; established by two controlled comparisons
  (\Cref{sec:fold-clostridia}), not inferred.
\item \textbf{Verified observation.} All $3\,200$ hard/safety rows are
  right-censored; the specialist predicts a lower bound on failure time,
  not failure time.
\item \textbf{Observed.} External literature evaluation on 21 cases gives
  $\rsq = 0.172$, MAE $39.4$ days, with all 21 flagged out-of-support.
\item \textbf{Unresolved.} Whether the generation rules share calibration
  across their shared components; whether the external cases are
  independent of the rules. Both require the generation pipeline, which is
  not in this repository.
\end{enumerate}
